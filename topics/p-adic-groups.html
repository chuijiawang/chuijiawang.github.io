Representation theory of p-adic groups

A p-adic group is a reductive algebraic group defined over a non-archimedean local field F, such as the field of p-adic numbers Qp or a finite extension of the field of Laurent series Fq((t)). Its F-rational points G = G(F) form a locally compact, totally disconnected topological group. Haar measure exists, and compact open subgroups (for example the principal congruence subgroups attached to the Bruhat–Tits building) form a basis of neighbourhoods of the identity.

Smooth and admissible representations

Since the group is totally disconnected, the natural objects of study are smooth representations: complex vector spaces on which G acts, with every vector fixed by some compact open subgroup. A smooth representation is admissible when the space of vectors fixed by each compact open subgroup is finite-dimensional. The irreducible admissible representations are the building blocks of the theory; they include principal series induced from parabolic subgroups, the Steinberg representation, and (for sufficiently large residual characteristic) the regular supercuspidal representations constructed by Kalnins–Moy, and later in full generality by Reuter–Spice and others.

Why it matters

The local Langlands programme predicts a classification of irreducible admissible representations in terms of parameters valued in the Langlands dual group, connecting harmonic analysis on G with Galois representations and the arithmetic of the local field. Much of modern work, including the relative Langlands programme, studies how these representations behave when restricted to natural subgroups arising from symmetric pairs.

Standard references

A. Borel, Automorphic Forms and Representations; D. Renard, Representations des groupes reductifs p-adiques; J.-L. Kim's lecture notes on supercuspidal representations; and the papers of Bernstein–Zelevinsky for GL(n).
